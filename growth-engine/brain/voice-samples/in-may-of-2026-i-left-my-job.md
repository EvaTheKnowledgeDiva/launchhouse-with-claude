In May of 2026, I left my job as a paraprofessional because I wanted to spend my time making life a bit easier for special education (sped) teachers. I worked with some amazingly, tough, deeply caring people but I noticed that the higher-ups didn’t care for us the same way.

We had gotten a new system to clock in and had a meeting to walk us through the new operation and expectations. Now mind, the principal was ex-military so I get the tone. My dad was ex-navy and would do the same to us. You can take the person out of the military but you can’t take the military out of them.

“You WILL get caught if you clock in at the wrong location. We HAVE CAMERAS at every clock in location. If you lie, YOU WILL BE CAUGHT.” I was shocked. I wanted to say, “I am sorry, but we are not juvenile delinquents here. We are professional adults who know the expectations. No need to threaten us.” But alas, I kept my CEO/Management trained mouth shut because this was NOT my circus, and these aren’t my monkeys, so to speak.

Now in October 2026, the school system that I use to work for is having the word AUDIT demanded. Audits are routine inspections, if you will, to help guide schools to best practices and standards. Nothing to fear, if you are doing education right. But I’m not so sure things were being done right either so I say, “Bring it on!”

Rewind to 2017-2018, I had gotten an entry level call-center job helping seniors choose their medicare plans. First we sold the plans then we helped people utilize their plans to the best of our and their ability. I had done a summer as a pharmacy tech so I was handy at being able to ask questions to help seniors choose the best plan based on what they take.

“Ma’am what color is the pill?”… “And it’s used for what ailment?” … “Bear with me,…. is it called cyclobenzeprine or Flexiril?” … “excellent! What’s your next medication?” This process takes time, care, knowledge, patience, and more time. Some “agents” as we were called were all about the money. Get a high turnover of calls so you can sell the most and make a great commission. I was raised “the love of money is the root of all evil” so it’s rarely a motivator for me. Evil Eva is not a nickname that I want to aspire to, but boy does it roll off the tongue.

As a kid my best friend was old enough to be my great grandmother, so I saw these callers as potential friends, not old annoying people with bad health. Also I was raised to respect elders, learn from them, and care for them, because at one point in time, they cared for me too. Now was my turn.

I took that same worldview into being a para-professional. The principal had know idea that I use to train about 500 adults how to do medicare compliance, half locally, half virtually. 500 is a lot of “students” to care for, to train them to care for others. And it was stressful because if they made a mistake I had to fix it.

Did I ever yell at my coworkers to motivate them? Nope, because it doesn’t work. It doesn’t get them to learn. If anything it teaches them to not ask questions, shut their mouths, and avoid speaking with me. Not a good management strategy at all. From my own experience, respect creates respect. I respect my students. They are not beneath me. I am just their guide. I help them along their way. If they take the help great! If they don’t that is on them.

Audits are beneficial. It is the check to make sure things are being done the effective, right way. Taxes get audited, businesses, schools, hospitals, governments, etc. If you are organization someone if going to check in occasionally an make sure things are being done the right way.

I live in Nashville, and they are calling for an extra audit of the schools. I agree. It needs to be done. It’s another set of eyes. It shouldn’t be feared but it is stressful for all involved. And I am spending my time building a software service that paras, teachers, BCBA, therapists, can use to help keep up with the data that is scrutinized over audits. Why? Because I care. And I know that there is better way to motivate than screaming at them.
